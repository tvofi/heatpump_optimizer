# Valley Geometry

A movement about the moment something heavy waits for a lighter hour. Form follows a quiet
economy: every mark is a step, and a step is either a cost or a saving. The work never shouts
its intelligence. It shows a profile of rising and falling ground and lets the warm thing
settle into the low places, the way water finds a basin. The whole grammar is a staircase and
what the staircase holds.

Space is organised on a strict modular grid, a lattice of two-unit steps where every edge,
radius and gap is a whole multiple of one unit. Nothing floats at an arbitrary offset. Forms
are orthogonal first and softened only at their joints, with radii that are equal everywhere,
so the eye reads one continuous hand. Negative space is structural. The valley is defined as
much by the emptiness above it as by the line that draws it, and that emptiness is guarded
with the patience of someone who has redrawn the same step a hundred times until it sat still.

Colour is two temperatures and a neutral, never more. A cool, deep fjord blue carries the line
of the world as it is: the price, the schedule, the constraint. A single ember warmth carries
what is chosen: the heat, the stored comfort, the thing moved in time. Neutrals are inked
navy and frost, calibrated so each pairing holds its contrast in daylight and in the dark. The
restraint is deliberate and costly. Each hue was tuned by measurement rather than taste alone,
the product of painstaking calibration until the warm and the cool could be told apart by any
eye, including one that cannot tell red from green.

Scale is rhythmic and self-similar. The same stepped profile must read as a glyph at sixteen
pixels and as a landscape at a thousand. That demands master-level execution at the smallest
size first: strokes that never fall below a pixel, gaps that never close, a silhouette that
survives being reduced to a favicon and still says one thing. Large compositions repeat the
step as a measured field of marks, dense and patient, like a chart from an imaginary
discipline that records the passage of cheap and dear hours with the gravity of a survey.

Typography is a geometric sans, set thin and tracked with care, used as architecture rather
than commentary. Words appear only where the form cannot speak: a name, a unit, a small
reference marker. Labels sit on the grid with the same unit spacing as the marks. The result
should look meticulously crafted, labored over for countless hours by someone at the absolute
top of their field: every alignment checked, every edge on the lattice, nothing touching that
should breathe, nothing crowded, nothing decorative that does not also carry meaning.
