## Revise point (one)

1. architecture-unsound -- R10-COP-GATE-D.md "Invariants" item 3, and the third bullet under "Alternatives": the claim that the solve and the arbiter passes after it read the same ruling is false on the tree. In `_async_update_data` (coordinator.py:5757-5796 at f40be9c97) the cycle solves first (`async_run_optimization`), then actuates, then runs `_record_accuracy`, which is where the draw fold lands. So a probe-routed fold that changes the store (the twelfth sample, or any median shift that moves L) lands *after* the cycle's solve. Every one-minute arbiter pass until the next solve (`pump_arbiter.apply` builds a fresh `arbiter_inputs()` on each tick, coordinator.py:3739-3746) then maps a plan booked on the old band against the new band. That is P2's failure case. Lapse is time-based, so it can flip the ruling on any tick. A failed or skipped solve keeps the old plan up to `_plan_is_stale`'s limit (three cycles, at least 90 min), and the mismatch lasts that long. Example: at lapse, a plan booked at L=3.0 has its 3.0 kW top step mapped on (1.0, 5.0), which writes 42.50 degC instead of 55.0, P2's own number. The proposed test ("a non-probe fold between the two leaves solve_band unchanged") never exercises the transitions that break the invariant. Fix wanted, one of: (a) map the plan by the band it was solved with: keep the solve's ruling (or its params' min/max) beside `_optimization_result`, and have `arbiter_inputs` pass that band, not a recomputed `solve_params(...)`; or (b) restate invariant 3 as bounded drift, give the bound (until the next successful solve, at most the stale limit), argue why that is acceptable in both directions (over-command at the twelfth sample, under-command at lapse), and replace the test with one that folds the twelfth probe sample and crosses the lapse between a solve and an arbiter pass. Either way, re-rule the rejected "carry the band on the plan's result" alternative, because its rejection rests on invariant 3.
