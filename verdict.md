## Block

1. **robustness: restore-record-stale.** `off_restore.async_restore` writes the neutral 0 to the offset entity on MODE_OFF and on unload, but `heat_curve`'s per-entry record (`state_for(entry)`: `written`, `written_at`, `misses`) is untouched, so after the restore the arm still believes the entity was told the planned value. `_STATES` is keyed on the config entry, so this survives an options-save reload as well as an off/on toggle. Measured with the reviewer's own probe (`ev/reviewer_probe_restore_record.py`, my instrument, not the finder's or the fixer's; inserted at the end of the R10 block and run through the fixer's `features_block.py` command, output `ev/reviewer-probe-run.txt`), through `async_publish_ecl110_command` and `async_set_mode`:
   - A, off then on inside 90 s of the last write, plan unchanged at 2: `writes=[2, 0]`. The 0 the restore wrote stays on the pump; the re-enable publish is held as "in grace" (W2), although the differing reading is the optimizer's own restore.
   - B, a first-miss rewrite that then held, then off and on, next cycle at the same target: `writes=[2, 2, 0]` and the warning `Heat-curve entity number.heating_offset does not hold 2.0 after a rewrite: not resent until the target changes`. W3 latches against a healthy entity, and the pump stays at 0 until the plan's integer changes, while the optimizer reports a displace of 2.
   - Null control B-null, the same history without the off/on: `writes=[2, 2] warn=[]`.
   The design's W2 says "a differing reading inside the grace proves nothing", and W3 is meant to catch an entity that does not hold. Neither is meant to fire on the optimizer's own restore write. Fix shape (one owner, no second writer): on a successful restore the consumer's write record is reset, e.g. `heat_curve` clears `state_for(entry)` (written/misses) when the restore it registered with sends its neutral, through a hook callback or by having the restore record the neutral as the write. A check is owed for both A and B.

## Findings

- Follow-up: the armed record is keyed by consumer key only (`held.armed[key]`), and the restore writes to the entity the *current* config names (`factory(config)`). A write armed on entity A, followed by a crash and an options change to entity B before the next start, restores B and leaves A displaced. For kind `offset` that is a neutral 0 to B, which is mostly harmless. For a consumer that has a baseline (the persistent kind, and the arbiter's set-point), it writes A's captured baseline into B. The persistent and arbiter consumers need the armed entity id persisted beside the baseline, with a restore to that entity or a disarm when it no longer matches. Carry: R10-GENERIC-HEAT-CURVE-PERSISTENT
