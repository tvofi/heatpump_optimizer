# D14 — Recurring bug classes

Owner's ask (Tim, round 8): *"an additional dimension, focusing on finding and
eliminating the most common bug classes."* Draft, **awaiting his
confirmation**: *"Recurring bug classes. Take the classes the programme keeps
re-finding, build the detector that enumerates every seam of each, and propose
the barrier that makes the class impossible."*

Read `COMMON.md` first; reading the ledger's earlier instances is the one
exception to its wall, recorded under `exposure`.

## Method

1. **Class ledger.** `tools/audit/bugclasses.json`; a class's `status` is
   `open`, `detector` (a command lists its seams) or `barriered` (a CI check).
2. **Pick** the top two or three `open` production classes by rounds present,
   then severity.
3. **Detector.** A harness under your round's `D14/` listing every seam in the
   package, not only known ones. It re-finds each listed instance (at pre-fix
   commits where it can), finds zero on a clean fixture, and moves under a
   one-line re-introduction.
4. **One finding per class**: the property, the detector command, every seam
   it lists, a proposed barrier.
5. **Barrier.** Where the detector lives permanently (a `tests/` script, a
   lint class, a runtime assertion) and its cost in gate seconds.

**Relation to `root-cause.md`.** It owns why a process let a class ship; at
three or more instances cite its record, if one exists, rather than duplicate it.

**Stop rule** (zero findings never comes): two consecutive rounds with no
`open` class gaining an instance and no new invariant break in the nightly
replay, and no high or critical production finding.
