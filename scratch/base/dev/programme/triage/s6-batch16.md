Instances: this is a rate, not a defect. From the orchestrator's account, not re-counted: unpinned sites went 4062 to 4012 between the 23 and 24 Oct scheduled runs while total sites grew 6283 to 6297, and the ledger recorded 20 to 28 kills a day over 18 to 24 Oct from 40 drawn. That is 3.65 wall-min, about 11 core-min, per mutant.
Premise correction (kept from the first ruling). Only `mutation-ledger` (`--drain`, then `mutation-ledger-push`) pins; `nightly.yml`'s own header says `mutation-nightly` "samples the whole package and records nothing". So every change here is on the ledger lane, and `mutation-nightly` stays at `--jobs 3 --max 40`.
What the tree already has (origin/main 2636ce9e9):
- `pin_shard(pool, k, n)` deals anchors round-robin in sorted order, so N shards are disjoint and together cover the whole pool.
- `merge_pin_shards(root, out)` (R9-CI-1, used by `mutation-autofix` over the `mutation-pins` matrix in `tests.yml`) folds per-shard `status`, `pins.json` and `head` with a status precedence.
- A `--drain` directory (`write_drain`) has that same layout plus `survivors.txt`.
What is missing is small. `--shard` is honoured only with `--pin-killed` (its help says so, and the `--drain` branch never slices). `merge_pin_shards` does not union `survivors.txt`. `nightly.yml`'s `mutation-ledger` is one job whose push downloads one artifact. And the pool-seconds cache is saved under `pool-seconds-${{ github.run_id }}`, a key N shards would collide on.
Cost: S, about 30 to 50 lines plus tests. In `tests/mutation_table.py`, `--shard K/N` with `--drain` takes `pin_shard` over `drain_pool`'s slice for the night's seed at a cap of N times `--max`, and the shard merge also unions `survivors.txt` (or a sibling built on the same fold). In `nightly.yml`, a `shard` matrix on `mutation-ledger`, a push job that downloads every shard's artifact and merges them before it applies, a pool-seconds save from one shard only (or a per-shard key), and the cron line. In `tests/entities.py`, one row pinning that the shards are disjoint and their union equals the unsharded slice for one seed (the null), and one pinning the merged `survivors.txt`. There is no paid cost: the repository is public (`private: false`), with free minutes and 20 concurrent hosted jobs, against 4 slots at about 00:55 to 05:00Z. There is no Pi or API cost.
Throughput: 4 shards times 80 is 320 drawn a night, with each shard estimated at about 220 min if 4 workers give the full 4/3 speedup, and clipped as partial at 240. At the measured kill share (20 to 28 of 40, about 60 percent), that is about 190 kills a night, so the roughly 3400 killable sites converge in about 2.5 to 3 weeks. The orchestrator's figure of about 270 a night assumes 15 percent survival, which the ledger's own numbers do not show.
Programme-CI interference (orchestrator's measurements, not re-counted): there is no runner contention. Merges by hour since 20 Oct were 12, 4, 6, 3 and 7 at 02 to 06Z, against 10 to 13 an hour in the afternoon. The cost is the one ledger push to main: one push run and possibly one train re-prove of about 30 to 45 min. Sharding keeps it to one push however many shards run, and the cron move puts that push in the quietest hours.
The cron move, checked against what shares it. `nightly.yml`'s one `schedule` starts `mutation-nightly`, `mutation-ledger` with its push, `slow` (150-min timeout), `nightly-ha` (45-min timeout) and `record-autofix`'s scheduled arm, and all of them move together; none depends on the hour. The other crons are separate workflows: `codeql.yml` (`41 2 * * 1`) and `governance.yml` (`0 7 * * 1`). `tests/entities.py` needs only a daily cron ("minute and hour fields and three stars"), which `37 0 * * *` keeps, and the seeds use `date -u +%Y%m%d`, so a 00:5x start keeps one seed per UTC day. The last two scheduled runs started 02:32:21Z and 02:35:26Z (15 to 18 min of dispatch delay). The R9 RCA (`dev/audit/rca/R9-NIGHTLY-MUTATION-BOUND.md`) once measured 5 h 54 m to 6 h 53 m, so the hour is a target, and the control reads `created_at`.
Risks, ruled:
- (a) Inventory staleness: all shards check out one commit of one run, and the push re-checks against the inventory (`stale_pins`), so this is no worse than today.
- (b) Survivor volume: about 40 percent of 320 is roughly 130 lines a night in `survivors.txt`. They are listed, not owed, and triage stays manual (`ci-autofix.md`), so this adds no work to any queue. But a surviving anchor stays in the stock, and once the killable part drains, the nightly draws mostly survivors and its yield falls. The shard count is therefore a knob: step N down to 1 when a night's kills fall under a quarter of its draw for three nights running.
- (c) Concurrency: 4 extra slots in the quietest hours, of 20.
- (d) Vacuous pins scale with volume, so R10-MUTATION-ANCHOR's attribution fix goes first.
- (e) Contention inside a shard: if 4 workers on 4 vCPUs give less than 4/3, each shard clips as partial at 240. That is never a failed job.
- (f) Timeouts: `driver_timeout` is 3 times the larger of the solo recording and the last pool cost (`pool_seconds`, measured at 3 workers), and a timeout is never a kill (#1880). The first night reports the timed-out count; on a rise, the fallback is `--jobs 3 --max 65` per shard.
Binding: an 8-fold nightly sample (40 to 320), converging in weeks instead of months, for about 40 lines that reuse `pin_shard` and the shard-merge fold, with no paid cost and still one push a night at the quietest hour. That clearly outweighs the added matrix. Every failure mode is a partial shard or a listed survivor, not a red main.
