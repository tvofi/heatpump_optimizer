# D11 — Governance mechanisms and policy

Owner's brief: *"Governance mechanisms and policy — audited against public
golden standards, with the goal of upholding an effective and high-quality
LLM and coding process, as well as maintenance and improvement of the
governance structure itself."*

Read `COMMON.md` first. Your subject is the process the other dimensions run
under, so you read what they are walled off from: the repository's history
and GitHub's own records — the ruleset, check runs, review comments, merged
pull-request bodies. You audit the mechanism, never the verdicts it
produced, and leave its cost and DORA's four keys to `D13.md`.

## Method

1. **Inventory the mechanisms from the tree and the API, not from the
   index's account of them.** One row per refusal a seat can meet: the
   ruleset on `main` and each required check with the `.github/workflows/`
   job that produces it; each check class `policy_lint.mjs` prints when run;
   `brief_lint.mjs`; the `tests/` gate (`tests/structure.py` among it); the
   claim files; `tools/pr/` (`prepr.sh`); `tools/plan/`; `tools/audit/seat/`
   with its merge train; the hooks in `.claude/settings.json`. Per row: what
   it refuses, where it runs (branch, `main`, both), the positive control
   that shows it alive, and what it leaves uncovered. The control is
   `node tools/policy/policy_lint_mutants.mjs` for a `policy_lint.mjs` class
   and the instrument's `--self-test` otherwise; a row with neither is one
   decision 0018 section 1 reads as NO-READ, and a finding, as is a
   mechanism whose control you cannot make fire. This inventory is the
   universe every later step reports examined over.
2. **Score against public standards, one executed check per criterion**,
   with the standard's current text fetched rather than remembered and its
   own wording as the bar:
   - OpenSSF Scorecard (https://github.com/ossf/scorecard): run it, or
     evaluate a check by hand where the tool cannot see this repository.
     Report per check with its reason string — `Branch-Protection`,
     `Code-Review`, `Dangerous-Workflow`, `Token-Permissions`,
     `Pinned-Dependencies`, `CI-Tests`, `SAST`, `Maintained`,
     `Security-Policy`, `Signed-Releases` — and never the aggregate.
   - SLSA (https://slsa.dev), Build track: the level the release path
     meets — `tools/release/stamp.py`, the tag, the artefact — and what the
     next level would require.
   - OpenSSF Best Practices Badge criteria on change control, test policy,
     release notes and vulnerability reporting.
   - NIST AI 100-1 (the AI RMF), its GOVERN function, and NIST AI 600-1, the
     generative-AI profile, read for a process in which LLM seats author,
     review and merge: accountability (is reviewer ≠ author provable from
     GitHub's records rather than from a comment's word), human oversight
     points, documented roles, defect and incident handling, measurement of
     the process itself. ISO/IEC 42001's continual-improvement clause is
     the bar for the second half of the owner's brief.
   - OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications, current edition: prompt injection,
     improper output handling and excessive agency, applied to every place
     a job or a seat executes or obeys text it did not write, a figure a job
     re-executes among them.
3. **Measure the structure's own health — the improvement half.**
   - Size and direction: `policy_lint.mjs --budgets` against its caps, and
     lines added per line deleted across the corpus over the window.
   - Recurrence: the friction histogram `policy_lint.mjs --stats` prints;
     a class past `CLAUDE.md`'s recurring-error rule with no root-cause record.
   - Conformance over every merged pull request of the window, enumerated
     as `D13.md` output 1 does. Per one: a `Fix review: merge` verdict at
     the merged head from a seat other than the author; a first
     `pr-contract` run at that head that was green (the commit's check-runs
     listing, which keeps the red run a summary drops); a disposition row; a
     red required check answered under `## Red checks`. One fraction per
     obligation.
   - Staleness where the `citations` class does not reach: status lines in
     the decisions against the tree, the traps in the handover,
     the files and steps the plan cites.
   - Duplication, over the whole policy set every round (tvofi 2026-11-15:
     *"policy files and policy related files should follow good data
     integrity practice, no duplication, that creats risks of divergence"*):
     a mandate is stated once, in the most authoritative file owning it, and
     pointed to elsewhere. Units: `{ git ls-files | node
     tools/policy/policy_lint.mjs --corpus-filter; git ls-files
     dev/governance/decisions dev/programme/RULINGS.md; } | xargs python3
     tools/audit/text_ledger.py units --dim D6`. One mandate in two files is
     a finding while they agree, and a higher one, quoting both, once they
     differ. Not one: a pointer, or a `.claude/rules/` copy
     `rules_sync.mjs --check` holds. A restatement recorded as deliberate
     (`CLAUDE.md` and `orchestrator.md` section 8) is reported with its
     reason, for the owner to rule.
   - The rulings file, which should shrink (tvofi 2026-11-15: *"This is
     exactly how I see it"*): entries, `awk '/^## Superseded/{exit} /^- /{n++}
     END{print n+0}' dev/programme/RULINGS.md`, and `wc -c`, against the
     previous round's baseline (none: the first reading). An entry policy
     now states is a finding, to drop; one older than a round still binding
     every session, a candidate to graduate, for the owner. Not one: the
     current mandate or round's direction.
4. **Perturbation**, per `COMMON.md`: an emptied check, a body with one
   digit changed. Never on `main`, never against the live ruleset, never a
   comment on a live pull request; a perturbation you cannot run marks the
   finding `provisional`.
5. **Mechanise first** — owner's brief addition, tvofi 2026-10-24: *"each
   operation or shape that that [sic] is not clearly a one shot and could be
   mechanical should be mechanical and wired into the machinery. This means
   reusable instrument before policy, only policy if no robust script could
   be built at a reasonable cost (tokens, time, complexity)"*. A finding
   goes to `proposal-triage.md` as a mechanisation proposal, costed in its
   terms and D13's; a non-finding records why none could be built at that
   cost.

The convergence scorecard (`tools/audit/seat/scorecard.py`) and round 11's
two measurement groups, R11-PRE-ROUND-METRICS and R11-PRE-TOKEN-LEDGER, are
measurements, and so exempt from the 20% bar `proposal-triage.md` sets on
machinery; audit whether each fires when it should, not what it gains.

## Output

Beside the report `COMMON.md` requires: the mechanism inventory; the
standards scorecard (criterion, standard, executed check, result, gap); the
conformance table; and a ranked list of changes, each with the standard it
moves and its cost in seconds per merge or lines of policy.
Severity: `critical` — a merge to `main` can happen with no review or with a
red required check, or a job executes text a seat did not write; `high` — an
obligation the record calls enforced that nothing enforces; `medium` — a
detector with no live control; `low` — hygiene.

## What has fooled people before

- The branch-rules endpoint is not bypass-aware: read the ruleset itself and
  probe it with both arms.
- A green mutation arm can be a mutation that never applied; drive the
  control by hand before reading the arm.
- The merge enumerator reads a free-text subject suffix (#677): compare the
  set of pull-request numbers against the API, not the totals.
