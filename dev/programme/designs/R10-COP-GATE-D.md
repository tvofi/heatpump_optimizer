## Owning seam and function boundaries

- **Owner, `draw_range.py`**: one new pure function *solve_band(draw, params, cap)*. It returns a frozen ruling with *max_kw* (`float | None`), *min_kw*, `source` and `reason`, plus `as_dict()`. It takes no coordinator. `planned_range` and `diagnostics_view` keep their behaviour. `planned_range`'s docstring ("a solve may book") is corrected to say diagnostics only: no solve has read it since it landed (22e16ea81).
- **The plan carries its band, `optimizer.py`**: `OptimizationResult` gains one field, *solve_band* (`dict[str, Any]`, default empty, which means the hub's band). This is the precedent `duty_floor_kw` already sets (`optimizer.py:1751-1754`, "the plan carries the floor it was solved under", read at `pump_arbiter.py:361`). `HeatPumpOptimizer` is not touched: the coordinator stamps the field.
- **Wiring, `coordinator.py`**. (a) `_solve_record` calls *solve_band(...)* once and passes the ruling to `_solve_hubs` (new keyword *band*). `_solve_hubs` builds the params copy from it, and the what-if caller (`coordinator.py:11001`) gets its own ruling there. `SolveRecord` gains a *band* field. (b) The one writer of the plan (`coordinator.py:6132`) becomes `self._optimization_result = replace(result, solve_band=record.band.as_dict())`. The ruling is the one computed before the await, so a fold during the solve cannot change it. (c) `_learning_view` publishes the plan's *solve_band* under *pump_max_power*, `None` with no plan. **`arbiter_inputs` is unchanged** and passes the hub's params and the plan, as today.
- **Map, `pump_arbiter.py`**: one function. `_band_kw` reads the planned min and max from `inp.plan.solve_band` when the plan carries one and the action is the plan's. For an experiment's action (`action["mode"] == "system_identification"`, CEILING-PROBE's routing key) it reads `inp.params`, the hub's, because sysid sizes its power on the hub (invariant 1). An empty band, or no plan, also falls through to `inp.params`, which is byte-identical to the base.
- **Publish, `sensor.py`**: `ModelStatusSensor` copies *pump_max_power* from the payload. `diagnostics_view` publishes CEILING-PROBE's raw store reading. The plan's ruling is the published reason, because the owner's ruling asks which top *the plan* used, and only the stamp knows that.

## The rule

Let `cfg_min` and `cfg_max` be the hub's configured figures and `L` CEILING-PROBE's raw median, which is `None` below twelve samples or 45 days after the newest sample.

| case | *max_kw* booked | `source` | `reason` |
|---|---|---|---|
| `cap.plan_writes_power()` | `cfg_max` | `configured` | `plan_writes_power` (the draw is the plan's echo) |
| fewer than twelve store samples | `cfg_max` | `configured` | *awaiting_probe* (+ sample count) |
| store lapsed | `cfg_max` | `configured` | `lapsed` (+ newest sample date) |
| `L >= (1 - AGREE_TOLERANCE) * cfg_max` | `cfg_max` | `configured` | *not_below_configured* (+ `L`) |
| `L < (1 - AGREE_TOLERANCE) * cfg_max` | `L` | *ceiling_probe* | *measured_below_configured* (+ `L`, date) |

*min_kw = min(cfg_min, max_kw)*. The bottom end never moves, the same rule `planned_range` applies on a duty-cycling install. The ruling never raises the top: a learned top above the configured one books the configured one. That books less heat, never fictional heat, which is CEILING-PROBE's own rationale. **Fallback** is every `configured` row: the solve's params are the hub's figures byte for byte. The published payload always carries *configured_max_kw*, *planned_max_kw*, `source` and `reason`, so a plan booking below the nameplate always shows why, and one that does not says which evidence is missing.

## Invariants (each one has a test)

1. **The hub never carries the learned top.** It goes only into the solve copy and the plan's stamp, never through `_model_corrections` and `_publish_params`. Probe P1: `DrawRange.observe` re-keys on the configured pair it is handed, so one fold keyed on `(1.0, 3.0)` cuts a 60-sample window to 1 (null: 61 folds on the configured key keep 61). The same rule keeps three things on the configured figures: `nameplate_power_floor_kw`, the COP duty floor, and the system-identification probe's commanded power (a probe at the learned top would measure its own clamp).
2. **The arbiter maps by the solve's band.** Probe P2: a 3.0 kW plan step mapped on the configured (1.0, 5.0) band writes 42.50 degC, not the 55.0 ceiling. On (1.0, 3.0) it writes 55.00 (null: 5.0 kW on the configured band writes 55.00). Without the stamp, the plan's top step never commands full power and the window's `full` evidence disappears.
3. **A plan is mapped and published by the band it was solved on.** Round 1 claimed that the solve and the later arbiter passes read the same ruling. That is false (verdict 47d510979): `_record_accuracy` folds after the cycle's solve (`coordinator.py:5760`, `:5779`, then the fold step), lapse is a clock, and a failed solve keeps the old plan up to `_plan_is_stale`'s limit (three cycles, at least 90 min). So the ruling is computed **once per solve**, stamped on the plan, and read back by the arbiter and the payload. Nothing recomputes it between solves, so no transition can split the plan's steps from their band. Probe P4 shows what a recomputed band would write, in both directions. At the twelfth sample, a 3.0 kW step solved on (1.0, 5.0) writes 55.00 degC on the recomputed (1.0, 3.0), an over-command, against 42.50 on its own band. At lapse, a 3.0 kW step solved on (1.0, 3.0) writes 42.50 against 55.00, an under-command (null: an unchanged band writes 42.50 on both). **Tests**, each through `pump_arbiter.apply` on the live coordinator and each with its null: (i) solve with eleven store samples, fold the twelfth, run the tick, and the written flow is the configured-band level, while the next solve books `L`; (ii) solve on `L`, advance the clock past the lapse without a solve, run the tick, and the written flow is still the `L`-band level; (iii) a failed solve after (ii) keeps the stamped band until `plan_stale`; (iv) `record.band`'s *max_kw* equals `record.params.max_electrical_power` and the stamp; (v) the published *pump_max_power* after (i) still names `configured` until the next solve.

## Alternatives, and why each lost

- **Publish through `_model_corrections` into the hub.** It lost on invariant 1: every fold would wipe the window and the latch (P1), and it would move the COP gates' nameplate figures, which is a threshold change.
- **Reshape `planned_range` into the one answer, latched window included.** It lost because it switches on a second behaviour no finding in this group names: the window clamp, including a raised bottom end on installs that cannot duty-cycle. It would also move 5 killed-mutant pins under `tests/mutation_ledger/killed_by/draw_range.py/` and 8 `tests/features.py` sites.
- **Carry the band on the plan's result: re-ruled, adopted.** Round 1 rejected it for two reasons. Its invariant 3 does not hold, and the builder line inside `HeatPumpOptimizer` (span 3408, so every line adds to `class_excess_loc`) is not needed, because the coordinator stamps the field at the plan's one writer. `OptimizationResult` spans 117 lines, under `GOD_CLASS_LOC_LIMIT` 300, so the field is free.
- **Fix (b), a bounded drift.** It lost on state ownership and call count. It keeps two computations of one fact, the solve's and the arbiter's. Their agreement depends on timing, and the bound is the stale limit, at least 90 min of P4's mis-mapped level in either direction. It also calls *solve_band*, a median over the store, on every arbiter pass: the cycle, every one-minute tick and the entity listener. Fix (a) calls it once per solve and reads a dict at each pass.
- **A coordinator attribute `_plan_band` beside `_optimization_result`.** It lost because it splits one plan's state across two attributes with separate writers, which every test and future writer must keep paired. It would also cost `coordinator_attrs`, measured 142 of 142.
- **`arbiter_inputs` passing a params copy with the plan's band applied.** It lost because choosing the band for an action (plan step against experiment) is a mapping decision, and mapping is `pump_arbiter`'s seam (CEILING-PROBE's owning-seam statement). The copy would also be a `replace` on every pass.
- **`min(store top, window top)`.** It lost because the window latch never releases within a configuration (`draw_range.py:19-31`, "So it latches for the configuration it judged"), so one low window top would outvote every later probe measurement.
