# R10-RCA-2205: the replay lane's cpu-doubled arm straddled its cap after the solve memo

The root-cause seat for #2205. It ran beside the fix (`handoff/r10-2205`, head
`d7bfeb0dd`) and not inside it (`dev/governance/roles/root-cause.md`).

**Defect.** The nightly Tests run 38530020347 (2026-10-14, main `9111acceb`, slow job
115739400655) failed one replay check: `cycle_cost sees a doubled cycle cpu`. The
cpu-doubled arm read `cpu_ratio` 3.5628 against the 3.576 cap; the clean arm read
1.8804. No production regression: the cycle got cheaper, not dearer.

## 1. Cause

Two conditions were both needed. Neither alone turned the check red.

1. **The cpu arm was never a doubling of what `cpu_ratio` times.** Since #1544
   (`bc0583545`, 2026-09-24), `inject_cost` spun inside `_async_update_data`. But
   `cpu_ratio` times the update, its executor jobs and the entity sweep. In all 37
   timed cost-lane runs on GitHub runners from 2026-09-25 to 2026-10-14 (20 scheduled
   and 17 dispatched slow jobs), hot/clean read **1.895 to 2.087**. The record commit
   `960681c12` printed the same shortfall without computing it: 4.550 hot against a
   clean of 3.576/sqrt2 = 2.529 is 1.80x. Reproduced at `9111acceb` on the seat box,
   three interleaved clean/cpu pairs: hot/clean 2.12, 1.95, 1.87.
2. **A merge window cut the clean cycle to 0.656x.** That window was #2142 (solve
   memo) and the merges after it. Four interleaved rounds of `tests/replay.py --one`
   per SHA on the seat box gave medians of 2.983 at `59b76606e` (the last green
   nightly) and 2.931 at `969cb109c` (#2142's parent). They gave 2.237 at `cb7cb25c7`
   (#2142: 0.763x) and 1.958 at `9111acceb` (0.656x in total). The fixer measured
   0.671x, which agrees.

The arithmetic: before the window, the lowest clean in 36 runs was 1.9147
(2026-09-27). With the lowest arm factor of 1.895, that is 3.628, above 3.576, so
all 36 were green. After the window, the runner's clean was 1.8804, and arm factors
of 1.87 to 1.97 give 3.52 to 3.70, which straddles 3.576. An exact doubling would
read 3.761 and stay green. **The under-doubling is the cause. The cheaper cycle only
exposed it.**

**Class search.** The other doubled arms are these. The memory arm reads the peak
directly, and its hot/clean in the same 37 runs is 1.994 to 2.006. The loop arm spins
`own_loop` on `thread_time`, which is the clock `loop_cpu_ratio` reads, so it is
exact. `tests/stress.py`'s `DETECTION_TARGET` sizes budgets and injects nothing.
The search found no other arm that perturbs a sub-span of the span its metric times.
The nearest record is `R9-N-cpu-gate-blind.md`. Its exact loop doubling read 2.85
against 3.576 and went unseen. That is the same cap, judged blind from the other side.

## 2. Process state: (c), followed and wrong

The process was the band check together with the doubled arm. Its stated premise is
in `tests/replay.py`'s `COST_DETECTION` comment: "the measurement sits in
(budget / 2, budget] and a doubled cycle is over it". The band check ran and passed,
because 1.8804 lies in (1.788, 3.576]. The arm ran. Each step was obeyed. The premise
that a clean reading in the band implies a red arm holds only for an arm of at least
2.0x, and this arm never reached that.

The state is not (d). #2142 moved a reading **inside** the band the design already
promised to cover, so the precondition it changed is not one the process relied on.

## 3. Cost test

`cost(countermeasure, recurring) < cost(defect) x P(recurrence)`, per release cycle.
The window is 2026-09-25T07:00Z to 2026-10-14T07:41:52Z (the red nightly's start), by committer date on main. It holds 19 stamps, about one per nightly.

- **Measured frequency.** 1 red in 37 timed cost-lane runs (0.027 per run). The
  triggering kind of event, a production speed-up large enough to move the clean
  reading near the floor, happened once in 19 release cycles (0.05).
- **Defect cost.** From the issue (2026-10-14T15:46Z) to the fixer's handoff
  (21:14Z) was 5.5 h of seat chain. `nightly-status` was red on main from 07:48Z and
  was still red at 22:12Z, at least 14.4 h. That red held record PR #2202.
- **The candidate countermeasure, a per-PR timed replay** of the cost arms on pull
  requests that touch the replay cost files, costs this much. The lane ran 38-128 s
  (20 nightlies), plus about 90 s of job setup. In the window, 60 of the 335 pull requests merged
  into main's first-parent history touched one of the five `COST_FILES`,
  `optimizer.py` or `tests/replay.py`, which is 3.2 per release cycle. That is about 10 runner-minutes per cycle, plus the timed lane's
  own false-red exposure on pull requests.
- **P(recurrence) after the fix is about 0 for this mechanism.** The fix spins
  exactly the clean span on the clock the metric reads. `short_doubling` checks that
  in every timed run, and its controls run on every pull request through
  `tests/entities.py`'s replay block. With an exact arm, a clean reading in the band
  implies a red arm, so the straddle cannot recur. What remains is a speed-up that
  leaves the band (clean below budget/2). That red is designed, and its message
  already says to re-record down. It costs one budget-lowering edit, with no
  investigation.

**Verdict: no separate countermeasure.** The class-eliminating change is the fix
itself: an exact arm, plus a check that the arm burnt at least its span. The per-PR
timed lane would cost about 10 runner-minutes per cycle against an expected cost of
about 0 for this class. It is refused for this defect. Whether the timed half should
move to pull requests is a separate design question, deferred in `tests.yml` "until
a week of real data has been timed". That condition now holds, at 37 runs. The
question belongs to the owner and is not motivated by this defect.

## 4. Findings for the fix (not built here)

**`cpu_ratio` 2.332 is off-centre.** The fixer centred it on five nightlies
(2.156-2.769). The 37-run record runs from 1.9147 to 2.8244. Projected by the
measured 0.656, that is 1.256 to 1.853, and the first post-window reading is 1.8804.
2.332 leaves 7.7 % above the floor and 24 % below the cap. The centred value,
sqrt(1.256 x 1.8804) x sqrt2 = **2.173**, leaves 15.6 % on both edges. It is a
lowering, which the ratchet permits.

**`loop_cpu_ratio` 0.426 cannot be re-centred without a raise.** The code did not
move it: on the seat box the medians are 0.319 at `59b76606e` and 0.313 at
`9111acceb` (0.98x). Its runner spread over the 30 runs that print it (the channel dates from 2026-10-01) is 0.2441-0.405 (factor 1.66), and the centre
is sqrt(0.2441 x 0.405) x sqrt2 = 0.445, which is a raise. The best value without a
raise is 0.426 as it stands, with 5.2 % top and 14.6 % bottom headroom. The spread is
the runner class, not the code. Across 30 runs, corr(loop_cpu_ratio, ref_ms) is
-0.94. On the 17 slow-class runners (ref_ms 52-54) the loop reads 0.2441-0.2783, a
spread of 1.14x. On the 13 fast-class runners (ref_ms 22-43) it reads 0.2957-0.405.
The BLAS-bound reference solve speeds up more than the pure-Python loop does. Lowering
the spread therefore means changing the normaliser, either to a reference with the
loop's instruction mix or to per-class budgets keyed on ref_ms. That is a measurement
change and the owner's call. No budget is raised here.
