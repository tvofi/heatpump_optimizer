# RCA-1985: the friction key `root-cause-unanswered` counts CI-found defects as process debt

The root-cause seat for issue **#1985** ("[policy] recurring friction:
root-cause-unanswered"), filed by the governance cron's friction step. It runs
beside no fix: the subject is the key itself (`dev/governance/roles/root-cause.md`,
`dev/governance/rules/defect-root-cause.md`). No production, workflow or policy
file is changed here.

- Base measured: `origin/main` at `3910026e` (2026-10-07).
- Window: `v6.7.16..origin/main`, the window the cron measured.
- Raw evidence is under `dev/audit/rca/1985/`. Every figure below names the
  file it comes from and the command that produced it (section 6).

**Trigger.** Recurrence: the stats histogram crossed its threshold of 3. This is
process friction and not a `bugclasses.json` class, so no class barrier is owed
and a refusal is a legal result (`defect-root-cause.md`, the cost test).

## 1. The count, reproduced

`node tools/policy/policy_lint.mjs --stats --since v6.7.16`, run with a token
(`stats_v6.7.16.txt`), prints the issue's line unchanged:
`root-cause-unanswered` at **12 PRs / 17 entries**, out of 57 merged PRs, of
which 50 carried a parsable verdict. The other keys over threshold in the same
window are `head-moved` 11/20 (#1990, another seat), `harness` 5/7 and `other`
3/4.

I enumerated the 17 entries myself by reading each merged PR's comments for a
`Fix review: blocked <sha> root-cause-unanswered` line (`entries.txt`,
`seq.txt`). That gives 12 PRs and 17 entries, the same as the tool: #1886,
#1942, #1948, #1957 (2), #1958 (2), #1959, #1960 (3), #1975 (2), #1979, #1980,
#1993 and #1996. The count is correct as a count of verdicts. What it measures
is the question.

## 2. Cause

### What the class was built to mean

`.claude/workflows/web-fix-wave.js:194-198` defines the class as *"a class that
means the fix is sound but the PROCESS owes an answer"*, and routes it to a
root-cause seat instead of a repair. `fix-review.md` step 11 is the only step
that reads the head's check runs. It sends **every** red the body does not
answer to this one word:
`blocked <sha> root-cause-unanswered: <check> went red, unanswered`.

### What the 17 entries actually were

For each entry I read every check-run attempt at the verdict head
(`commits/<sha>/check-runs?filter=all`, `timeline_all.txt`). I then read the
PR's next verdict (`seq.txt`).

| measure | entries | PRs |
|---|---|---|
| the verdict names at least one lane that tests the fix's own code or tooling (typing, mutation, closures, fast, env-matrix, CodeQL, policy-docs, wave-script, graders-head-copy, coverage-ratchet) | 13 | 10 |
| the verdict names only procedural reds (`budget-raise-gate` awaiting the owner, `nightly-status`/`delivery-status` grading main) | 4 | 3 (#1942, #1948, #1975) |
| the next verdict is `merge` **at the same head** (body-only repair) | 4 | 3 (#1942, #1975, #1979) |
| the head changed before the next verdict | 13 | 9 |

"Live NameError" (#1960), "81 unpinned sites" and "3 new un-dispositioned sites"
are in the verdict lines themselves. In these cases the reviewer was blocking a
broken head. The `## Red checks` silence was secondary to that.

### Why the body was silent: the reds arrive after the handoff

- `fixer.md` step 6: the push of `handoff/<topic>` with its body **is** the
  handoff. `orchestrator.md`: *"Dispatch a fix's reviewer when the fixer
  pushes, not when CI settles"* (881c375b, 2026-09-16).
- The `Tests` workflow took 28 to 45 minutes at these heads. For example,
  #1957: created 20:26, completed 21:03. #1960 `455db64e`: 07:16 to 08:01.
- In **12 of 17** entries, the first `pr-contract` run at the verdict head was
  `success` (`timeline_all.txt`). The body passed the contract when it was
  handed off and only became non-compliant when CI's reds landed later.

So any red that CI alone produces is, by construction, unanswered at handoff.
Step 11 then files it under `root-cause-unanswered`, whether it is a process
debt or the fix's own defect.

### The cheaper detector exists, and it fired every time

`pr-contract` refuses a body that does not name a red check
(`policy_lint.mjs` `checkPrBody`, about 20 s). `pr-contract-rerun.yml` re-runs
it when a watched workflow concludes red. Across the 17 entries:

- **11** verdicts were issued while `pr-contract` had **already failed** at that
  head. The first failure came between 1 and 50 minutes before the verdict.
- **6** verdicts (#1957 ×2, #1958 ×2, #1959, #1960 `455db64e`) were issued
  **before the `Tests` workflow completed** (`Tests` done 21:03, 01:24, 03:03,
  01:40 and 08:01 against verdicts at 20:52, 01:07, 02:26, 01:37 and 07:33).
  The rerun triggers on workflow completion, not job completion, so
  `pr-contract` could not have re-run yet. A reviewer may verdict an unsettled
  head, because `fix-review.md` has no settle requirement.
- **0** entries where the reviewer was the only detector after CI settled.

**Measurement trap.** The default `check-runs` listing (`filter=latest`)
showed `pr-contract` green at #1993 and #1996 and hid three failed attempts of
each (runs 37538098127 and 37536298810, attempts 1 to 3, all `failure`). My
first pass misclassified both from that listing. `fix-review.md` step 11
already warns against a one-run-per-check listing, and this confirms the
warning.

**Named cause.** `fix-review.md` step 11 keys every red the body does not
answer to the single class `root-cause-unanswered`. The body is necessarily
silent on every red CI produces after the push-time handoff. So the friction
key counts CI-found defects in the fix (13 of 17 entries) together with real
process debt (4 of 17), and reports the total as process friction.

## 3. Process state: **(c)**

The process was followed:

- the fixers answered what existed at handoff (the first `pr-contract` run was
  green in 12 of 17 entries);
- the reviewers applied step 11 exactly as written;
- the cheaper detector fired on every red (11 before the verdict, 6 at
  workflow completion).

It did not produce the intended result. The intended result is a key that
isolates "the fix is sound but the process owes an answer". What it delivered
mixes 13 broken-head blocks into that key. This is not (b): no instruction was
disobeyed, and a firmer "name your reds" cannot name reds that do not exist yet
at handoff. It is not (a): both step 11 and `pr-contract` exist.

**(d) was tested and is not established.** The rate rose sharply. Counting PRs
by merge date (`verdicts_since_0916.txt`), PRs with at least one
`root-cause-unanswered` verdict were:

- 2026-09-16 to 2026-10-01: **11 of 370** verdict-carrying PRs (3.0 %);
- 2026-10-02 to 2026-10-07: **27 of 97** (27.8 %).

The candidate precondition change was 6b1ec3b9 (2026-09-30, *"reviewers cite
CI's heavy runs"*). Before it, CI reds would have surfaced under the reviewer's
own measuring classes. But broken-head entries already appear before that
commit: #1559 (typing, mutation), #1561 (fast) and #1563 (typing), all on
2026-09-24. So the conflation is older than the commit. I do not attribute the
rate rise to it. Round 9's completion volume is the unmeasured alternative.

## 4. How far the class reaches

1. **The web wave's route.** `web-fix-wave.js:489-495` sends a
   `root-cause-unanswered` verdict to a root-cause seat and returns `blocked`
   **without a repair round**. Two problems follow:
   - In 13 of 17 entries the head needed a change. Under that script, the group
     would have stopped with no repair.
   - Even in the 4 body-only entries, `fixer.md` ("Before you hand off: answer
     any check your branch turned red") makes the body answer the **fixer's**
     job. The root-cause seat owes only the analysis.

   Round 9 ran these PRs through the orchestrator by hand, which sends blocked
   verdicts to the fixer. So the route was not exercised in this window. It is
   live code that would strand a group. This is owed work for the orchestrator
   (section 5, item 3). I filed no issue.
2. **Earlier windows.** 11 PRs from 2026-09-16 to 2026-10-01 carry the same
   mixture:
   - #1077, #1108, #1110, #1116 and #1165 are `pr-contract`-only, which is
     genuine body debt;
   - #1418, #1559, #1560, #1561, #1563 and #1732 name typing, mutation or fast
     lanes.
3. **The typing lane is a separate known class.** 4 of the 17 entries name
   `typing`. Its local detector (`tests/typing_ruler.py` under
   `HPO_TYPING_PYTHON`) is optional by design (#1091, #1099, #1095;
   `gate-scoping.md`). That is the cheaper-detector question for those reds.
   It belongs to their own fixes' `## Red checks` answers, not to this key.

## 5. Cost test and decision

Both sides are in wall-clock per 3-day window, the window this cron measured.

**Rework that this key's process actually causes.** This is the body-only
repairs: the next verdict was `merge` at the same head.

- #1975: 21:26 to 21:31, 5.7 min.
- #1979: 23:09:52 to 23:14:09, 4.3 min.
- #1942: no second verdict was posted. It merged at the blocked head
  `0e2ee62b` 12 min after the block.

That is about **22 min per window**. The other 13 entries' rework is the repair
of a real defect. That repair would have happened under any verdict word,
because `pr-contract` (required) and the red lane itself block the merge.

**Countermeasures considered:**

1. **A check-shaped detector** (a settle gate in `bus.sh push-verdict`, or a
   prepr prediction of reds known at handoff).
   - A settle gate does not change the count. The 6 premature verdicts would
     be the same blocks, issued later.
   - A prepr prediction could cover only the procedural reds known at push:
     `budget-raise-gate` on a raise, and main's `nightly-status` /
     `delivery-status`. That is 3 PRs and about 22 min per window. Its
     standing cost is about 1 to 2 s per prepr run, plus a build and review of
     a few seat-hours for at most 22 min per window saved.

   **Refused.** The cheaper detector, `pr-contract` with its rerun, already
   exists and fired on 17 of 17. No added check moves this number.
   **Revisit** if body-only `root-cause-unanswered` repairs (same head, merged
   on the next verdict) reach 3 PRs in one window. That number is directly
   measurable from `seq.txt`'s shape.
2. **Split the grammar: proposed to the owner, not built (policy).** This
   addresses state (c).
   - Add one verdict class, e.g. `red-check: <check>`, for a head red on a
     failure the fix itself must repair.
   - Keep `root-cause-unanswered` for "the fix is sound, the body is silent on
     a red".
   - Touches the `VERDICT_CLASSES` literal (which `policy_lint.mjs` reads),
     `fix-review.md` step 11, and `orchestrator.md:248`.
   - Standing cost: **0 s**, since it is a word in a list.

   **Effect on this window:** the key falls from 12 PRs to the 3 body-only PRs.
   That is still at the threshold of 3, so the cron would still file. The 10
   broken-head PRs would key as `red-check`. That key would also cross its
   threshold, but it would name the lanes (typing, mutation, closures). The
   next seat would then be pointed at CI-found defects that the local gate
   missed, which is the real recurring cost. In hours the change is about
   neutral: a seat is still dispatched per window. Its value is that the seat
   is dispatched at the right subject. **The owner decides.** I recommend it
   because its standing cost is zero, but the gain is direction, not hours.
3. **The web-wave route** (section 4, item 1). The fix is that
   `root-cause-unanswered` dispatches the root-cause seat **and** a body-repair
   round. It is owed by whoever next edits `.claude/workflows/web-fix-wave.js`.
   It is recorded here as owed work for the orchestrator's record.

**Disposition for #1985.** The count is reproduced and correct as a count of
verdicts. Its attribution is refuted: 13 of 17 entries are broken heads that CI
found after the handoff, not body debt. No check is built. The grammar split is
pending the owner, so this analysis does not by itself discharge #1985.
