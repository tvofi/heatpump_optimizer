# Codebase audit, August 2026

A full review of the integration at v3.7.1: correctness, physics, algorithm
optimality, architecture and maintainability. Twelve reviewers swept the
codebase independently (control theory, thermodynamics, numerical
optimization, Home Assistant integration practice, config-flow UX, frontend,
time handling, learner statistics); every non-trivial claim was then
adversarially verified against the code and, where it mattered, against
simulation before any fix was written. 26 substantive findings survived
verification; all were confirmed real. Everything below lives on the audit
branch as sixteen fix commits, each with its own detailed message.

## How to read this branch

Each commit is self-contained, validated, and explains its finding, its fix
and its blast radius. The commit messages are the detailed audit record; this
document is the map. Suggested review order is chronological (`git log
--reverse`) — config surface first, then optimizer, then physics, then
learners, then tidy.

**Golden fixtures need a re-record on your machine.** Several fixes
deliberately change planned behaviour, and the committed fixtures do not
reproduce bit-exactly across scipy/numpy versions in any case (this
environment produces last-decimal solver differences on a clean checkout of
main). After reviewing, run `python tests/golden.py --record` locally and
read the diff against these expectations:

- **Optimizer commit (`3b3f4b2`)**: DHW trickles disappear (schedules become
  real blocks or zero); force-off pins are honoured by the planners.
- **PV commit (`1c83b5f`)**: the PV scenarios move hot water into surplus
  hours; `coord_all_features.forecast_prices` shows raw import prices, not
  the old cliff repricing. Most other scenarios move at the last decimal
  (cost-sum reassociation perturbs the line search).
- **Wind commit (`e357f82`)**: essentially all scenarios re-plan — baseline
  and predicted costs drop in windy scenarios, `config_flow` default 0.15 →
  0.03.
- **Baseline commit (`932b0fd`)**: `baseline_cost` and savings move in most
  scenarios (the reference now follows the day/night schedule); plans shift
  slightly where the baseline seeds the solver.
- **Tidy commit (`7fef6f4`)**: `config_flow` loses the dead
  `heat_pump_entity` field. Nothing else moves.
- All other commits leave the fixtures byte-identical (verified against a
  locally recorded baseline at each step).

## The headline findings

**PV pricing repriced whole steps on trivial surplus** *(critical —
`1c83b5f`)*. The old formulation substituted the export price for a step's
entire price whenever any surplus existed, so 0.05 kW of winter sun made a
6 kW compressor draw look nearly free. Consumption is now priced piecewise
(surplus-covered energy at export, the rest at import) in both objectives,
in the hot-water planners' rankings, and in every reported cost figure.

**Your open question — why the optimizer will not charge the tank — is
answered** *(diagnosis in `docs/backlog.md`, item 29 status block)*. Two
parts. The valve's unset-target fallback was `house_temp + 1.0`, a receding
target that never throttles — fixed on this branch (`64b6194`). The
remaining and decisive part: the model discharges the tank valve-wide-open
at the raw tank temperature (~26 kW into 21 °C rooms from a 45 °C tank), so
every candidate plan relaxes to the same discharged tank before the horizon
ends and the terminal credit has no gradient with respect to charging power.
The optimizer is not refusing to charge; the model tells it storage does not
work. Suggested fix in the backlog: bound discharge by emitter output at the
*mixed* flow temperature, and make the valve P-gain dt-invariant.

**Radiator-house presets modelled an unheatable house** *(`024e204`)*. The
model pushes every watt through the "slab" store; a radiator preset gave
that store the building's slow mass behind a 0.05 kW/°C transfer, so
delivering 3 kW needed the emitter 60 °C above the room. The slab keys now
describe the radiator circuit and the slow mass coasts in the room store.
Separately, a floor-heated two-zone lower floor counted the slow mass twice.

**The wind default overstated wind loss ~5×** *(`e357f82`)*. 0.15/m/s claims
+150% heat loss at 10 m/s; infiltration studies support 0.02–0.04. Every
windy forecast panic-charged the house and the heat-loss learner spent weeks
compensating. Now 0.03, with a README table for tightness/exposure.

**Zero-mean sensor noise ratcheted the learners** *(`4086791`)*. One-sided
rejection (house scale) and off-centre clamping (zone-split ratio) turned
symmetric noise into monotone drift — measured 1.0 → 1.2 in 60 simulated
days at σ = 0.1 °C. Both now clip each sample's target symmetrically about
the current estimate. Same commit: both interval learners attributed the
*next* plan's power to the elapsed interval; COP-scale and defrost-derate
learning is now disjoint by the frosting band instead of correcting one
shortfall twice; the learned COP scale survives restarts; the DHW usage
learner no longer books standby cooling as phantom draws in all 24 hours.

**Everything aligned by position instead of by clock** *(`31878dd`)*. A
stale price list shifted the whole horizon a day; Tibber's 15-minute prices
would each stretch to an hour; a stale weather forecast read hours out of
phase; capacity-tariff windows folded from the solve instant instead of the
DSO's hour, halving a burst's priced excess; the price prior's level was
biased by wherever the known window ended. All five now align by timestamps.

**The savings baseline ignored the user's schedule** *(`932b0fd`)*. The
reference thermostat held the flat target around the clock while the plan
followed the day/night schedule, silently booking the night setback to the
optimizer. The reference now follows the same schedule. Notably this
*raises* baselines in winter: a dumb setback thermostat recovers at full
power straight into the morning price spike, and dodging that spike is real
optimizer value the old reference hid.

## The rest of the fixes, by commit

- **`95f495d` config flow**: options pages only clear their own entity
  fields (saving one page wiped entities configured on others); both DHW
  forms enforce the setpoint/minimum deadband.
- **`11e2496` services**: full range validation on every service field,
  `ServiceValidationError` throughout, the ECL110 displace bounds admitted,
  day-window sanity per entry, all seven services unloaded.
- **`37f48c4` entities**: cost sensors are statistics-valid
  (MONETARY/TOTAL/SEK — they can go down when savings do), a crash guard on
  the observed-COP sensor, bulky schedule attributes excluded from the
  recorder, the away binary sensor's wrong device class dropped.
- **`1a4a201` card**: dragging a slot no longer pops the slot dialog on
  release; the dialog's font sizing survives re-renders.
- **`63a4eca` config flow (v3.7.1 regression)**: the mixing-valve page owns
  and clears its own target entity; v3.7.1 had left it in the shared
  entities page where the cross-page wipe resurrected it.
- **`3b3f4b2` optimizer**: manual force-off pins reach the LP and greedy
  planners as bounds (overlaying them afterwards deleted energy the tank
  was counting on); the safety-release scan covers the whole trajectory;
  min-run repair no longer ping-pongs with the capacity clamp; the terminal
  refill price scales with `price_weight` and uses the same mean-COP
  convention as the settlement; cycling and capacity terms scale with
  `price_weight` in both objectives.
- **`939f589` away/learners**: away setback also lowers `target_temp` (the
  pull-to-target term ignored the band-only setback); recovery time is a
  full-power ramp through the real model instead of a fixed rate; presence
  polarity honours device classes and textual states; the external-heat
  detector's sample window scales with the update interval (it was
  permanently blind at 60-minute updates); comfort-weight learning only
  counts nights with a real price spread and real heating; buffer-cooling
  learning freezes in valve modes (house draw contaminated every pump-off
  sample).
- **`64b6194` mixing valve/thermal model**: default valve target is the
  comfort ceiling (see headline); the buffer cap clamps the *rate* so an
  over-cap tank no longer teleports to the cap in one step, deleting heat.
- **`7fef6f4` + `bd06b71` tidy**: dead code deleted (the `get_state_matrices`
  trio from your backlog shortlist, five other dead functions, two never-
  emitted reason codes, 83 dead constants, the ignored `heat_pump_entity`
  config option); `upper_floor_area_ratio` became a real mapped parameter
  (it was configurable and silently ignored — internal gains always split
  50/50); the PV production entity now overrides the current step (it was
  configurable and ignored); the Optimize Now button visibly disables
  during a solve; simulated steps carry the full state; docstrings now
  describe the objective the code actually minimizes.

## Deferred findings (recorded, not fixed here)

Design-level items that deserve their own change rather than riding an
audit branch:

1. **Tank discharge law** — the item-29 answer above; the single highest-
   value change available. Sketch in `docs/backlog.md`.
2. ~~**One-sided settlement**~~ — **fixed**, though not for the reason given
   here. The settlement is now symmetric, with `caps` keeping a credit honest by
   limiting every store to the temperature above which its heat is of no further
   use.

   "Biases mildly against storing" is wrong on both counts.
   `_deferred_energy_cost` feeds `predicted_savings` and the sensors and reaches
   the objective nowhere — the objective prices its own end state through
   `_terminal_cost` — so it could not bias a plan, and the fix changes none: of
   37 golden scenarios, 2 move and both move only `deferred_energy_cost`,
   `predicted_savings` and `savings_percentage`, with every power schedule and
   trajectory byte-identical.

   What it did bias was the reported number, and not mildly. Measured across ten
   scenarios, three ended with more useful heat than the thermostat baseline and
   were credited nothing: shoulder understated by 8 %, flat prices by 24 %, and
   flat prices **with no mixing valve at all** by 62 % — the building's own mass
   ending warmer than a thermostat would have left it, which has nothing to do
   with storage.
3. **System identification fits a first-order plant** — the step-response
   experiment fits one time constant to a two-store (room + slab) plant, so
   its `heat_loss_kw_per_c` blends envelope loss with slab charging. Its
   confidence-weighted blend limits the damage; a two-exponential fit would
   fix it.
4. ~~**Economy mode is auto mode**~~ — **fixed.** Confirmed literally before it
   was changed: with the real coordinator and identical frozen inputs, auto and
   economy produced the same power schedule to 0.0 kW, the same predicted cost
   to thirteen decimal places, and differed in exactly one published key. It now
   widens the comfort floor by 1.5 K (never below 15 °C), measuring −24.6 % on
   winter_typical, −21.0 % on winter_moderate, −18.9 % on winter_narrow and
   −4.5 % on flat prices.

   The suggested stronger price weight was measured and rejected: it is the same
   degree of freedom the comfort learner owns and would be erased by it, it does
   nothing at all in the two winter profiles where a savings mode is most
   wanted, and it raised the bill 29 % on the shoulder profile. Two companions
   shipped with it, without which economy was not a state a user could hold —
   the mode is persisted across reloads, and the optimizer switch no longer
   forces auto over a live selection.
5. **`SolarRadiation` vs `SolarIrradiance` sensors** duplicate one reading
   under two names; removing one is a breaking change users notice, so it is
   flagged rather than done.
6. **`last_buffer_trajectory` statefulness** — the objective records it and
   the terminal cost reads it within one call; correct today, fragile against
   refactors that reorder the two.
7. **`_current_humidity`** still reads forecast entry 0 positionally; the
   defrost bucket tolerates a stale-by-hours humidity, but it could reuse the
   timestamp alignment for free.

## Validation

Every commit was validated before push: `features.py` (282 checks, grown
from 254 by the audit's regression tests), `entities.py` (133),
`manual_plan.py` (68), `edge.py`, `solar_alignment.py`, `open_meteo.py`,
`frontend.py`, `card.mjs`, `validate.py` (18 seasonal scenarios, no
issues), and a golden-fixture diff against a locally recorded baseline at
the preceding commit so each commit's behavioural footprint was inspected in
isolation. The critical guards are mutation-tested: reverting any of the
five learner fixes, the PV cliff, the trust regions or the alignment fixes
fails a named check.
