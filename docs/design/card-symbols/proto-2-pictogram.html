Design review note — concept 2

Mechanism
Every box type gets a 14-unit pictogram inside the box, centred above the title on its own line: open tray under a cloud for outside air, cabinet with a fan for the heat pump, domed cylinder for a store, chamfered block with a bowtie for the valve, radiator and hatched slab for the two floors. The wood tank's pictogram is the cylinder with the spring that the re-design removed from the drawing, restored at symbol size.
Cost
17 viewBox units of header height on every box: the tallest column grows by about 51 units (294 → about 345 in the measured fixture), the price of a second header row. The title keeps its full width, so the valve's 165.6-unit title still fits the 168-unit inner width.
Risk
Two header rows slow the title scan, and eight pictograms at 9–11 px on a phone are decoration unless the titles are read. It also reintroduces a small version of the silhouette vocabulary the re-design dropped, so the card must own that vocabulary deliberately rather than grow it ad hoc.
At 375 px
Pictograms are about 11 px at the card's 560 px phone floor — readable as shapes, not as detail. The extra header line costs no horizontal room, so the three-column drawing is unchanged under sideways scroll.
Wood-coil answer
The strongest of the five: the coil is a thing you can see on the tank, not a badge on a corner. The pictogram uses the card's removed helix language; the hot water box still says the coil state in words, and the coil pipe is the one accent pipe.
Configure affordance
As concept 1 — the two-line Configure button names its options page inside the folded block, and the page caption gives the Settings path.
