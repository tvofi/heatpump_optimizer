=================== D0.md
# D0 — Price optimality

Owner's brief: *"Price optimality. Specifically hunt for sub-optimal planning."*

Read `COMMON.md` first. This brief says what to measure and how; the contract
there says what counts.

## What you are hunting

Plans the shipped solver returns that a better search finds strictly cheaper
on the **same objective, bounds and inputs**, with comfort and hot-water
feasibility no worse. Sub-optimality has three known homes: the seeding and
multi-start (which basin the starts land in), the budget (iterations, function
evaluations, tolerance), and structure the solver cannot see (bang-bang
schedules, the DHW/space decomposition, the terminal credit, horizon effects).
You are not asked whether the objective is the right objective — that is D2 —
but whether the solver minimises the one it has.

## Method

1. **Capture harness.** `mock.patch.object(optimizer_module, "_multi_start_minimize", ...)`
   (the idiom in `tests/optimality.py`) to record every call's objective,
   candidates, bounds, args, `maxiter` and result, then race challengers
   against the exact recorded objective. Score every plan with the production
   objective, never with energy price alone: a challenger that is cheaper on
   energy and worse on comfort pull or cycling is not cheaper.
2. **Challengers**, cheapest first: more L-BFGS-B starts (structured seeds:
   bang-bang at several energy fractions, greedy-cheapest-hours, the
   thermostat baseline, the previous cell's plan); larger `maxiter` and
   `maxfun`; tighter `ftol`; a different `eps`; warm-starting L-BFGS-B from
   the best challenger; a global method (`scipy.optimize.differential_evolution`
   or a dynamic program on a coarsened grid) as an outer bound on the same
   objective. Report the gap between production and the best feasible
   challenger, and the gap to the outer bound.
3. **Feasibility parity** before any "cheaper" verdict: per-step comfort floor
   and ceiling, DHW availability in windows, power bounds, manual pins.
4. **The grid**: every price profile × weather profile in `tests/profiles.py`
   (8 × 5), topologies single/two-zone, DHW on/off, valve storage, wood,
   horizon 6/24/48 h, and the coordinator captures. Report per cell; the
   aggregate needs leave-one-out and the most-favourable-cell drop.
5. **Null control**: the same race at `profiles.prices("flat")`. A gap that
   survives flat prices is not price optimality; name what it is. A real
   finding that is out of dimension is carried per `finding-propagation.md`.
6. **MPC masking**: for every cell with a gap, does the step-0 action differ?
   If re-planning at the next step reaches the same trajectory, the gap is
   not realised. Simulate the receding horizon over a day for the cells that
   matter (`tests/rolling.py:run_rolling` shape).
7. **Decomposition**: does iterating DHW ↔ space more than once change cost?
   Does the terminal credit change the plan the way its docstring claims?

## Reuse

`tests/optimality.py` (`setup`, `evaluate`, the patch idiom), `tests/golden.py:make`
and `SCENARIOS`, `tests/backtest.py:score` and its strategies,
`tests/stress.py:build_case`, `tests/profiles.py`.

## Traps

- Timing under the fan-out is provisional; function-evaluation counts and
  iteration counts are your evidence.
- The batched gradient serves some bound shapes and not others; a race
  must use the same `jac` path production would (capture it).
- `_MULTI_START_SOLVES` and the pre-refinement scoring decide which
  candidates get refined at all; count what is discarded.
- Report SEK/day only beside the flat-price arm and the bill share; a 1 %
  gap on a 60 SEK day is a finding, a 1 % gap on a 2 SEK summer day is not.

# D1 — Robustness and stability

Owner's brief: *"Robustness and stability — lifecycle, staleness, executor
boundaries, store corruption, guards."*

Read `COMMON.md` first.

## What you are hunting

Ways the integration wedges, leaks, races, publishes stale or fabricated data
as measured, or turns a corrupt input into a permanent failure — under real
Home Assistant semantics, not the test stub's.

## Method

1. **A real-loop harness.** Subclass `tests/harness.py:FakeHass` so
   `async_add_executor_job` runs in a `ThreadPoolExecutor` on a real
   `asyncio` loop, and `async_create_task` schedules instead of closing.
   Then setup → first refresh → reload mid-solve → unload mid-solve → setup
   again. Assert no live task, listener, timer or executor future references
   the torn-down coordinator (`asyncio.all_tasks`, `gc.get_referrers`), no
   exception escapes, and the new instance's first cycle completes.
2. **Store corruption fuzzing.** Enumerate every persisted payload
   (`Store(`, `async_load`, `async_save`, snapshots, ledger, learners,
   manual plan). Per store, at least 200 seeded mutants of the healthy
   payload: type swaps, missing keys, `NaN`/`inf`, negative or huge numbers,
   wrong nesting, truncation, strings where dicts go. Load through the real
   loader, run one cycle, assert it completes, the corrupt part is quarantined
   or reset with one log line, and the *next* cycle does not repeat the failure.
   Add one perturbation to the store-fuzz set: an instant written by a clock ahead of
   the one that reads it back, driven across a restart.
3. **Staleness.** Drive the watchdog and every "age" sensor under: a frozen
   clock jump forward/back, DST transitions (`tests/hastub` `dt.freeze`,
   `tests/dst_checks.py`), `last_reported` in the future, states `unknown`
   and `unavailable`, a price list that stops updating, a weather entity
   that returns an empty forecast.
4. **Executor boundary.** From `_solve_record` and `_solve_hubs`,
   list every `self._*` the solve thread reads and every loop-side writer of
   them; a race harness mutates each writer mid-solve and checks the plan and
   the published payload for torn state.
5. **Guards.** Every `try/except` that swallows: does it log once, does it
   set `last_update_success`, does it recover on the next cycle, does it
   hide a bug that should surface? Executed by injecting the exception.
6. **External-input parsers.** Every parse of input the integration did not
   produce — ECL110 MQTT, pump-signal states, the Tibber and Open-Meteo
   responses, the weather forecast — driven with malformed, non-finite,
   oversized and hostile payloads: the cycle completes, the failure logs once,
   the arm is pinned. #1090 made the ECL110 handler drop a NaN or malformed
   payload whole, logging each drop at DEBUG; drive the parsers it left.

## Reuse

`tests/harness.py`, `tests/entities.py:_honest_coordinator`,
`tests/golden.py:_capture_coordinator`, `tests/hastub/homeassistant/util/dt.py:freeze`.

## Traps

`FakeHass` serialises the executor and closes coroutines. Real HA runs setup
and unload through the config-entry state machine, one at a time per entry; a
race reproduced only by calling a lifecycle method directly is a stub artefact.
=================== D2.md
# D2 — Mathematical and physical sanity

Owner's brief: *"Mathematical and physical sanity — the optimizer's objective,
the thermal model, COP and derates, tariff and price arithmetic."*

Read `COMMON.md` first.

## What you are hunting

Equations that are wrong, units that do not cancel, identities that do not
hold, and physical bounds that are violated — each shown by an executed
identity with a stated tolerance, not by reading the code.

## Method

1. **Thermal model.** Energy conservation per step (stores' enthalpy change
   equals delivered heat minus losses, to 1e-9 kWh); dt-invariance (four
   15-minute steps versus one hour, with the tolerance the integrator's
   order predicts); scalar `simulate_step` versus `simulate_trajectory_batch`
   bit parity on space-only, DHW, valve, wood, coil, external-heat and PV
   inputs; monotonicity (more power never cools a store); the buffer cap
   clamps a rate, not a state; no store crosses a physical bound in any golden
   scenario.
2. **COP and derates.** COP monotone in outdoor and flow temperature, Carnot
   fraction within the stated band, every derate in [0, 1], defrost derate
   bounded and continuous at the band edges, capacity envelope never below
   demand-at-derate without a reason code.
3. **Objective.** `predicted_cost == Σ price·power·dt` on every golden
   fixture; the savings identity; the terminal credit's sign and magnitude
   versus a re-simulated continuation; every penalty term's scaling with
   `price_weight`; the gradient used by the solver versus a central finite
   difference at random points (relative error and where it is worst).
4. **Tariff and prices.** Folding 15-minute and hourly prices into steps,
   month boundaries, 23- and 25-hour DST days, the capacity tariff charging
   the monthly peak exactly once, grid-fee day/night and weekday windows,
   currency conversion and the PV export price applied piecewise.
5. **Estimators.** `sysid.identify` bias under white, quantised (0.1 °C) and
   drifting noise; the confidence gate versus the bias it admits; the COP and
   heat-loss learners' clamps.

## Reuse

`thermal_model.py:simulate_step/simulate_trajectory/simulate_trajectory_batch`,
`compute_cop`, `tests/golden.py:SCENARIOS`, `tests/dst_checks.py`,
`tariff.py`, `grid_fee.py`, `price_model.py`, `tests/profiles.py`.

## Traps

Five fixtures are non-reproducible across BLAS builds (the `may-drift` set);
do not report last-ulp differences there as findings. An identity that holds
at one operating point and fails at another is the finding — sweep.

# D9 — CPU and memory efficiency (Raspberry-Pi-class target)

Owner's brief: *"CPU and memory resource efficiency — target hardware is
Raspberry-Pi-class running all of Home Assistant. Measure, don't assert:
express findings as relative cost (share of a solve, allocations per step,
bytes retained, renders per gesture), include the harness, and state the
extrapolation wherever a Pi conclusion is drawn. A finding without a
measurement is not a finding. Priorities: the gradient cost (batched jac,
96 simulate-step-equivalents per L-BFGS iteration, not ~9,300); the longest
contiguous GIL-holding interval (two 2 ms yields sit between L-BFGS starts);
how many full solves a cycle actually runs; the DHW planning loops; per-cycle
loop work outside the executor; retained collections with no trim; published
payload size versus the recorder exclusion set; and whether the stress budget
should become a real gate — CI sets no `STRESS_*` variable, the ms budget is
retired, and `rss_peak_mb`/`traced_peak_mb` record all 51 scenarios."*

Read `COMMON.md` first. You have an isolated worktree for instrumentation.

## Metric definitions (fixed, so two agents cannot score different things)

- **Simulate-step-equivalents per gradient** = scalar `simulate_step` calls
  + rows of `simulate_trajectory_batch`, both hooked by monkeypatching the
  production symbols, per gradient evaluation of `_multi_start_minimize`.
  Report for: the default two-zone DHW solve, the single-zone solve, the
  zero-range-bounds fallback (a manual pin; a DHW block consuming the whole
  cap), and each DHW planning loop.
- **Full solves per cycle** = entries into `_multi_start_minimize` during
  one coordinator cycle, split by path (main solve, shadow/what-if,
  diagnose, any other).
- **Longest contiguous GIL hold** = the maximum gap between 1 ms heartbeat
  ticks of a task on a real event loop while the solve runs in a
  `ThreadPoolExecutor`; **starvation share** = the fraction of the solve's
  wall time during which the gap exceeded 5 ms. Never on `FakeHass`.
- **Loop-thread work per cycle** = `time.thread_time()` on the loop thread
  across one cycle, excluding the executor.
- **Retained bytes** = `tracemalloc` and deep `sys.getsizeof` of every
  coordinator collection after N cycles, as a slope per cycle, plus
  `ru_maxrss` in a subprocess.
- **Payload bytes** = JSON bytes of `_build_data_dict` and of every entity's
  attributes, split by what `_unrecorded_attributes` excludes, per cycle
  and per day.

## Method

1. One harness file per metric under your round's `D9/` (round 2's at `d5d8c4a`;
see `harnesses/README.md`), each with the header the contract requires, each hooking a named symbol and moving under
a named perturbation (e.g. `maxiter`, horizon length, cycle count). Use
`tests/stress.py:reference_solve` as the ruler for every "share of a solve".
2. Then: does the stress gate as shipped detect a synthetic 2× regression
(inject work into the solve and run its checks)? What can it not see?

## Extrapolation

Every Pi statement carries the factor and its source (state it as an
assumption if it is one) and the M1 number it scales from.

## Traps

Timing during the fan-out is provisional; counts, bytes and CPU ratios are
final. Pin threads before numpy; print `thread_factor`. The two existing
`sleep(0.002)` yields sit between L-BFGS starts and between the DHW and
space stages, not inside an iteration.

# D13 — Process yield and cost

Owner's brief: *"a governance structure that enforces this and has good first
pass yield."*

Read `COMMON.md` first. D11 audits the mechanism: does a refusal fire when it
should. You audit the outcome: what the process pays per merge, and how often
a pull request passes first time. A check that does not fire is D11's finding;
a check that fires correctly while the process still pays N rounds for it is
yours. You read the same sources D11 does — `main`'s history, check runs,
comments, reviews, bodies — from a worktree with `.git`, each recorded under
`exposure`; the verdicts' content is your population, never your evidence.

## Required outputs

Each with its rule beside it and its enumerator named; every count derived at
your own window, none carried from a document or a comment.

1. **First-pass yield and rounds per merge.** Population: merged pull
   requests whose merge commit is first-parent in the window, from
   `/commits/<sha>/pulls` — the API mode of `policy_lint.mjs`'s
   `enumerateMerges` — with `api_failures` printed beside the count. A
   verdict is an issue comment *or* a pull-request review whose first line
   matches `^Fix review:\s*(merge|blocked)`, ordered by time. Yield is the
   share whose first verdict is `merge`; rounds are verdicts per merge, mean
   and max.
2. **Verdict coverage by class.** Merges with no parsable verdict, split by
   title prefix and author: fix and feature, record and chore, bot cycles,
   other lanes. A class is exempt only by a rule you can cite beside the
   row; no rule in the tree exempts record pull requests today.
3. **Block classes, three ways.** Every `blocked` verdict by the class in
   `web-fix-wave.js`'s `VERDICT_CLASSES`, then bucketed engineering /
   record-and-body / orchestration; the bucketing is yours, stated in the
   harness header. A class the grammar does not have is its own row.
4. **DORA's four keys, by job.** `dev/audit/rounds/round4/D11/dora_keys.py` is
   the base; its change-failure rate is per *workflow*. Re-take it per check-run
   name from `d11lib.py`'s `check_runs`, then without the jobs on an exclusion
   list of by-design reds, a JSON file beside `policy_budgets.json` that your
   first round creates with a reason per job. A merge also fails if a `main`
   commit descending from it says `This reverts commit <sha>` of it or of a
   commit it merged, or a merged `Revert "…"` pull request does.
5. **CI seconds per merge, governance against the gate.**
   `governance_cost.py` and its `GOV` set, re-derived from the workflow files
   at your window: jobs were added since it was recorded.
6. **The friction histogram's keys.** A rule id `k`, `#` anchor dropped, names
   each `--budgets` path that is, or ends in `/` and, `k`, `k` plus `.md` or
   `k/SKILL.md`: one path is that file, several (`README.md`) an ambiguous row.
   Report the share naming one file, and per file each spelling's count.

## Perturbation

Perturb in a fixture, never on a live pull request: one verdict comment
re-shaped out of the grammar lowers coverage by one; one job added to the
exclusion list moves the failure rate by exactly that job's count; one accepted
entry's `ratchet-budgets.md` re-spelled `README.md` moves to the ambiguous row,
and as `ratchet-budgets` or unbackticked moves no file's count.

## Severity

`high` — a measured cost exceeds what the mechanism it pays for catches, e.g.
more review rounds spent on body figures than on any engineering defect over
the window; `medium` — an instrument that cannot compute its own metric.

## What has fooled people before

- The grammar is `web-fix-wave.js`'s `VERDICT_RE`. A hand-written verdict can
  fall outside it (`Fix review: PASS — merge <sha>` did); `--stats` prints it
  as outside the grammar. Count it on its own row, never fold it in silently.
- `policy_lint.mjs`'s `fetchWindow` reads issue comments only. A verdict posted
  as a pull-request review is invisible to it and to that endpoint; read
  `pulls/<n>/reviews` too and say which endpoint each verdict came from.
- `--paginate` on an object endpoint concatenates page objects; decode each
  and join their `check_runs`, as `d11lib.py` does.
- A count of merges compares the set of numbers against the API, never totals.
- A matrix job's check-run name carries its leg (`fast (3.14)`); keying on the
  bare job name counts zero. A skipped run's duration is negative; sum only
  runs that concluded.
- `actions/runs` caps at 1000 results and silently truncates a longer window;
  per-commit check runs have no such cap.

# D15 — Debugging run from a live diagnostics bundle

Owner's ask (#1938): *a debugging run from a diagnostics data export*.
Read `COMMON.md` first.

**Inputs**, at absolute paths outside every tree, named in your task:

- **Primary:** the "Download diagnostics" file's `debug` bundle with its

- **While the download's `replay` is null** (until R10-DIAG-REPLAY):

- **Null control:** `tools/replay/dbg_bundle_gen.py`'s synthetic bundle;
  every step runs on it first.

**No real bundle: every step `unfinished`, no finding**; a synthetic-only
anomaly is a lead.

## Method

1. **Intake and version seam.** Unwrap `debug`; `integration_version` is the download's, not each row's. A restart gap is consecutive `cycle_rows[].t` at least three `optimization_interval`s apart (the download's `config`, else 30 min). Releases install on their date: a cycle ran the newest tag (`git for-each-ref refs/tags`) dated before its last gap; a tag after that gap leaves it unknown, never the older. Attribute each anomaly so and re-check it at the baseline; old-version-only is a non-finding, with evidence. A gap is downtime, not a lead, unless the integration ran through it.
2. **Privacy gate.** `tests/replay.py`'s `LEAK_PROBES` controls green, then `export.violations` over the bundle with a planted-credential arm; either failing stops the run.
3. **Ingest.** `tools/replay/debug_ingest.py --bundle`; `--corrupt accuracy` is its perturbation; a check passing on an empty section is vacuous.
4. **Store replay.** Each store document through its loader and `store._sanitize` against `store.DOMAINS`: quarantined leaves, refused versions.
5. **Timeline replay.** `tools/replay/debug_replay.py --bundle`, stores seeded, over the replay half: the lane's judges, offenders per cycle.
6. **Monitor re-run.** Bias and MAE per window, stated; `Cusum` and `SnapshotRing.observe_bias` against the recorded `accuracy_drift` latch.
7. **Row scan.** `cycle_rows` and `payload_snapshots`: solve failures, `solve_wall_ms` outliers, stale feeds, action against plan, each self-test re-derived.
8. **Anomaly to seam.** Each step-7 anomaly to its `tests/seam_map.json` seam, reproduced by `debug_replay.py --start/--end` over its window, with the perturbation that moves it.

**The bar.** The replay judges, `debug_ingest.py`'s checks and each step's
re-derivation are the graders. A finding is re-run by one
command against the bundle path, and moves under its perturbation.

**Privacy.** No bundle value is written unless `export.violations` passes it.

**Out of scope.** Store fuzzing (D1), missing tests (D3), class detectors
(D14): leads.
